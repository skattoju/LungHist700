\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Lung cancer histopathology classification is a clinically relevant computer vision problem where automated tissue interpretation can accelerate screening and improve diagnostic consistency. Our project evaluates four approaches on LungHist700~\cite{lunghist700paper} at 20$\times$ magnification in a three-class setting: adenocarcinoma (ACA), normal (NOR), and squamous cell carcinoma (SCC).

We aim to (i) reproduce the ResNet50 patch-level CNN baseline, (ii) reproduce an end-to-end MIL pipeline, (iii) implement two frozen-backbone MIL aggregators (multi-head self-attention and gated attention), and (iv) quantify and explain the gap between supervised patch-level and weakly supervised bag-level approaches.

% =========================
\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Dataset.}
LungHist700 comprises 691 high-resolution (1200$\times$1600 px) histopathological lung images at 20$\times$ and 40$\times$ covering seven sub-classes grouped into three superclasses. Patient-wise splitting (no ID overlap across train/val/test) prevents leakage, and class-weighted cross-entropy addresses class imbalance.

\paragraph{CNN Baseline.}
A ResNet50 backbone pretrained on ImageNet is fine-tuned for 3-way classification. Images are downscaled to 300$\times$400 and randomly cropped to 224$\times$224 patches during training.

\paragraph{End-to-End MIL.}
Each slide is treated as a \emph{bag} of 20 randomly sampled 224$\times$224 patches from the full-resolution image (matching the paper's bag construction~\cite{lunghist700paper}). A shared ResNet50 backbone encodes each patch into a 2048-dim embedding; a 4-head self-attention module aggregates patch embeddings before a linear classifier. The entire network is trained jointly under bag-level supervision.

\paragraph{Frozen-Backbone MIL.}
To address end-to-end training instability, we freeze the ImageNet ResNet50 and pre-compute embeddings for 35 deterministic grid patches (5$\times$7) per image. Two lightweight heads are trained on these fixed embeddings: (a)~\textbf{Multi-Head Self-Attention} — 4-head self-attention with mean-pooling; (b)~\textbf{Gated Attention}~\cite{ilse2018} — per-patch sigmoid-tanh importance scores with weighted-sum aggregation.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
We report Accuracy, Macro-F1, and ROC-AUC on the held-out test set, with per-class breakdowns via normalized confusion matrices and epoch-wise learning curves.

\paragraph{CNN Baseline.}
The fine-tuned ResNet50 achieves \textbf{91.7\% accuracy and AUROC 0.995}, matching the paper's ~90\%/98\% benchmark. NOR classification is near-perfect; the dominant error is ACA/SCC confusion, consistent with overlapping morphological patterns. Training converges in $\sim$30 epochs with no severe overfitting.

\paragraph{End-to-End MIL.}
The jointly trained backbone + self-attention model reaches \textbf{70.8\% accuracy}. Our accuracy is $\approx$10 points below the paper's MIL figure; however, our \textbf{AUROC (0.913) exceeds the paper's (0.89)}, indicating well-calibrated class rankings despite noisy hard predictions. The gap is attributable to: (i)~the cold-start problem (attention and backbone updated simultaneously); (ii)~gradient noise from 25M parameters supervised by a single bag label; and (iii)~a small training set ($\approx$560 bags).

\paragraph{Frozen-Backbone MIL.}
Both frozen aggregators match or approach the end-to-end MIL accuracy while training orders of magnitude faster (no backbone gradients).

\textbf{Gated attention} (Acc 70.8\%, F1 0.700) outperforms self-attention on every metric. Its per-patch sigmoid-tanh gating is better regularised than multi-head self-attention given only $\sim$560 training bags. NOR precision reaches 93\% and ACA recall 83\%. SCC recall (55\%) is the weakest link.

\textbf{Multi-head self-attention} (Acc 68.75\%, F1 0.647) underperforms due to overfitting: its higher parameter count disadvantages it in the small-data regime, and SCC recall collapses to 36\%.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{CNN $>$ MIL, as expected.}
Our results confirm the same ordering reported in Marino \etal: the patch-level CNN outperforms all MIL variants by $\sim$20 points in accuracy. Patch-level labels provide a dense supervision signal — every forward pass is labelled — while bag-level labels create a much noisier gradient. This advantage disappears in practice when patch-level annotations are unavailable, motivating MIL.

\paragraph{End-to-end MIL AUROC gap.}
Despite lower accuracy, the end-to-end MIL achieves the highest AUROC among MIL variants (0.913 vs.\@ 0.866 gated). Joint backbone training, though unstable, adapts the feature extractor to histopathology, improving the ranking of class probabilities even when hard boundaries shift. This makes end-to-end MIL more suitable for risk-stratification tasks.

\paragraph{Frozen backbone — gated $>$ self-attention.}
The ImageNet domain gap limits both frozen models' AUROC ($\sim$0.86 vs.\@ 0.913 end-to-end). Within the frozen paradigm, gated attention's inductive bias (sparse, independent patch suppression) outperforms self-attention's relational modelling on this small dataset. SCC is the hardest class across all MIL settings, indicating that ImageNet ResNet50 features poorly encode squamous-cell morphology.

\paragraph{Bag construction.}
The paper uses 20 stochastic crops per image; our end-to-end MIL matches this exactly. The frozen variant uses a deterministic 5$\times$7 grid (35 patches) for reproducible offline extraction, providing exhaustive spatial coverage at the cost of including more uninformative tiles.

\section{Challenges and Reproducibility}
\label{sec:attempts}

Environment issues (CUDA/cuDNN library resolution under WSL, TensorFlow–protobuf alignment, and missing dataset path defaults) were resolved to enable stable training. A critical MIL bug — patches extracted from a downscaled 300$\times$400 image rather than the full 1200$\times$1600 original — was corrected to restore micro-texture information. All code and trained weights are available at \cite{skattoju}.

\section{Future Steps}
\label{sec:future}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Domain-adapted backbones:} replace ImageNet ResNet50 with UNI, CONCH, or PLIP to close the ImageNet domain gap and improve SCC recall.
\item \textbf{Vision Transformer extension:} evaluate ViT (default and fine-tuned) as an alternative backbone.
\item \textbf{Interpretability:} Grad-CAM overlays for CNN and attention-weight heatmaps for MIL to validate that salient regions correspond to clinically meaningful features.
\item \textbf{Statistical validation:} report mean $\pm$ std over multiple random seeds to quantify variance in the small-data regime.
\end{enumerate}
