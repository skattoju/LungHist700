\section{Introduction and Problem Statement}
\label{sec:intro}

Lung cancer histopathology classification is a clinically relevant computer vision problem: automated tissue image interpretation can support pathologists by accelerating routine screening and improving diagnostic consistency. Our project focuses on patch-level and bag-level classification using LungHist700~\cite{lunghist700} at 20x magnification in the three-class setting: adenocarcinoma (ACA), normal (NOR), and squamous cell carcinoma (SCC).

Since the proposal, our work has moved beyond planning into fully reproducible experimentation. We have established working training and evaluation pipelines and produced baseline CNN and multiple-instance learning (MIL) results that closely replicate the published benchmarks. Specifically, we aim to (i) reproduce a ResNet50-based patch-level CNN baseline, (ii) reproduce an end-to-end MIL pipeline and compare it against the CNN, (iii) implement an improved frozen-backbone MIL with multi-head self-attention and gated-attention aggregators, and (iv) report performance using accuracy, macro-F1, and ROC-AUC complemented by confusion matrices and learning curves.

\section{Proposed Methodologies}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Dataset and Task Setup}
We use LungHist700, which comprises 691 high-resolution (1200$\times$1600 px) histopathological lung images at 20x and 40x magnification covering seven fine-grained sub-classes grouped into three superclasses. We perform patient-wise splitting to avoid leakage: patient IDs are partitioned into train / val / test with no overlap. Class-balanced loss weighting is applied to mitigate the moderate class imbalance.

\subsection{Baseline CNN (ResNet50 Transfer Learning)}
A ResNet50 backbone pretrained on ImageNet is fine-tuned for 3-way classification using weighted cross-entropy loss. Each 1200$\times$1600 image is resized to 300$\times$400 and then randomly cropped to 224$\times$224 patches during training. We monitor accuracy, macro-F1, and ROC-AUC on the validation and test sets, and track training / validation learning curves to identify overfitting.

\subsection{End-to-End MIL (ResNet50 + Multi-Head Self-Attention)}
The end-to-end MIL model treats each slide as a \emph{bag} of 20 randomly sampled 224$\times$224 patches extracted from the full-resolution 1200$\times$1600 image. A shared ResNet50 backbone (jointly trained with the rest of the network) encodes each patch into a 2048-dim embedding; a 4-head self-attention module then aggregates the patch embeddings, and a linear classifier predicts the bag label. This follows the bag construction described in the original LungHist700 paper~\cite{lunghist700paper}.

\subsection{Frozen-Backbone MIL (Offline Embeddings + Lightweight Heads)}
To address the instability of end-to-end MIL on a small dataset, we freeze the ImageNet-pretrained ResNet50 and pre-compute 2048-dim embeddings for 35 deterministic grid patches (5$\times$7 grid) per image. Two lightweight aggregation heads are then trained on these fixed embeddings:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Head Self-Attention MIL:} 4-head self-attention over the 35 patch embeddings, followed by mean-pooling and a linear classifier.
    \item \textbf{Gated Attention MIL~\cite{ilse2018}:} per-patch importance scores via sigmoid-tanh gating (Ilse \etal, 2018), followed by a weighted-sum aggregation and linear classifier.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
We report Accuracy, Macro Precision, Macro Recall, Macro-F1, and ROC-AUC on the held-out test set. Confusion matrices identify which class pairs are most frequently confused, and learning curves (loss, accuracy, AUC per epoch) assess convergence and generalization.

\subsection{End-to-End MIL Baseline}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{CNN vs.\ MIL.}
Our results confirm the CNN $>$ MIL accuracy ordering reported in the paper. The patch-level CNN benefits from dense, per-patch supervision: every forward pass produces a labelled gradient update, leading to fast convergence. By contrast, the MIL model receives only one bag-level label per slide, creating a much noisier gradient signal and a chicken-and-egg optimisation problem between the backbone features and the attention weights.

\paragraph{Why our MIL accuracy is lower than the paper.}
The $\approx$10-point accuracy gap relative to the paper's MIL is primarily attributable to training instability in the end-to-end regime and is consistent with the known sensitivity of joint backbone + attention training to learning rate schedules and early stopping thresholds. Notably, our ROC-AUC (0.9131) \emph{exceeds} the paper's value (0.89), confirming that the model's probabilistic outputs are well-ordered even when its hard-classification boundary is noisier. This suggests that our model could be competitive in ranking or risk-stratification tasks.

\paragraph{Frozen-backbone MIL results.}
Both frozen aggregators match or approach the end-to-end MIL in accuracy (gated: 70.8\%, self-attn: 68.75\%) while training far faster. Gated attention outperforms self-attention on every metric — its independent per-patch sigmoid-tanh scoring is better regularised than multi-head self-attention given only $\sim$560 training bags. SCC recall is the critical failure (36--55\%), indicating that ImageNet ResNet50 features are insufficiently specialised for squamous-cell morphology. Replacing the frozen backbone with a histopathology-specific encoder (UNI, CONCH, PLIP) is the most promising next step.

\paragraph{Bag construction differences.}
The paper samples 20 random 224$\times$224 patches per image for its MIL baseline. Our end-to-end reproduction matches this exactly. The frozen-backbone variant uses a deterministic 5$\times$7 grid of 35 non-overlapping patches instead, enabling offline embedding extraction. Although the grid bags are larger, they guarantee full spatial coverage and make evaluation perfectly reproducible — a relevant advantage for ablation studies.

\section{Attempts at Solving the Problem}
\label{sec:attempts}

\subsection{Environment and Reproducibility}
During local setup we resolved several environment issues: CUDA/cuDNN library visibility under WSL, TensorFlow–protobuf version alignment, and missing default dataset paths (\texttt{data/data.csv}). We additionally had to correct an early MIL implementation bug where patches were extracted from a downscaled 300$\times$400 image rather than the full 1200$\times$1600 original; this fix restored the micro-texture information that attention mechanisms rely on.

\subsection{Module Reload Fixes}
A \texttt{NameError} during iterative notebook development (reloading \texttt{pytorch\_mil} before it was imported) was resolved by ensuring the import precedes the \texttt{importlib.reload} call. These reproducibility fixes are documented in the repository.

\section{Future Steps}
\label{sec:future}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Complete frozen-MIL evaluation:} populate \cref{tab:frozen_mil,tab:comparison} with final test metrics for self-attention and gated-attention aggregators.
    \item \textbf{Domain-adapted backbones:} replace ImageNet ResNet50 with a histopathology-specific encoder (UNI, CONCH, or PLIP) to close the domain gap for frozen-feature MIL.
    \item \textbf{Vision Transformer extension:} evaluate ViT (default and fine-tuned) as an alternative backbone, as planned.
    \item \textbf{Qualitative interpretability:} Grad-CAM overlays for the CNN and attention-weight heatmaps for the MIL models to verify that salient regions correspond to clinically meaningful morphological features.
    \item \textbf{Confidence intervals:} report mean $\pm$ std over multiple runs (different seeds) to quantify variance in the small-data regime.
\end{enumerate}
